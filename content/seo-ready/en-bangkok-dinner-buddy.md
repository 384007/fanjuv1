# Bangkok Dinner Buddy | Fanju app

Bangkok is one of Southeast Asia's most vibrant food cities, and finding the right dinner companion can transform a meal into a memorable social experience. Fanju connects people who want to share a table, discover new restaurants, and build genuine friendships over food.

## What Is a Dinner Companion in Bangkok?

A dinner companion is someone you share a meal with — not a date, not a business contact, just a person who enjoys good food and good conversation. In Bangkok, where the food scene ranges from street stalls to rooftop restaurants, having a dinner companion opens up the full range of dining experiences.

## Who Joins Bangkok Dinner Gatherings?

- Expats new to the city looking to meet locals
- Digital nomads wanting real-world social connections
- Locals who enjoy meeting people from different backgrounds
- Travelers staying longer than a week who want authentic experiences

## How Fanju Works in Bangkok

1. Browse open dinner gatherings in Bangkok
2. Submit a basic profile for host review
3. Join a small-table dinner at a public restaurant
4. Meet your dinner companions in a safe, structured setting

## Why Small-Table Dinners Work

Large group events can feel impersonal. Fanju focuses on tables of 4–8 people, where conversation flows naturally and everyone gets a chance to connect. Bangkok's diverse restaurant scene provides the perfect backdrop.

## Safety and Trust

All Fanju dinners take place at public restaurants. Hosts review guest profiles before confirming seats. There are no hidden fees or surprise venues.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Fanju only for expats in Bangkok?**
No. Both locals and expats join Bangkok dinner gatherings. The mix of perspectives is part of what makes the experience valuable.

**Do I need to speak Thai?**
Most Bangkok dinner gatherings on Fanju are conducted in English, though some are bilingual. Check the gathering description for language details.

**How many people are at a typical dinner?**
Most gatherings have 4–8 guests plus the host, keeping the table small enough for real conversation.
