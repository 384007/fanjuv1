# Bogota sustainability talk is cheap until you live above 2600 meters with real waste and water problems | Fanju app

Bogota talks a lot about sustainability on paper and at corporate events, but the actual daily reality for most residents is altitude headaches, diesel fumes that never fully clear in the valleys, and neighborhoods where trash collection is irregular and water pressure drops for days. A real Sustainability Dinner in this city is not about ESG slides or reusable straws. It is about people who actually deal with these constraints sitting down and saying what works and what is performative bullshit.

Fanju app brings small tables of people together over food for exactly this kind of conversation. It is not a dating app, not a random group chat, and not an endless scroll. In Bogota it is one of the few ways to cut through the greenwashing and hear from the people who live with the consequences of bad infrastructure and altitude every single day.

## The altitude + traffic + trash combination that kills most imported sustainability ideas

Most "green" solutions that work in Medellín or coastal cities die in Bogota within six months. The altitude messes with batteries and composting rates. Rush hour traffic on the mountain roads means electric vehicle fantasies hit range anxiety hard. Informal settlements on the hillsides have waste pickup that is weeks behind, so separating organics at source sounds nice until the dog or the rain gets to it. Anyone who has actually tried to run a real composting or recycling project here will tell you the first three things that broke.

## Zona G performative dinners vs the actual barrios where the waste problem lives

A sustainability dinner in a quiet Zona G restaurant with good wine and nice lighting will mostly attract people who want to be seen caring. The people who actually manage waste cooperatives in Suba or Usme, or who run small repair shops that keep plastic out of the river, rarely have time or money to sit in those rooms. The ones who do show up to a real small table are usually the ones who have already failed at three projects and can tell you exactly why the next trendy imported solution will also fail. That is the only conversation worth having in this city.

## Water cuts, diesel in the valleys, and why most expat green projects get ignored by locals

Bogota has real water rationing in certain sectors during dry seasons. The poorer neighborhoods feel it first and longest. Meanwhile the main roads are choked with old diesel trucks and buses. When an expat or a corporate sustainability person shows up talking about carbon offsets or bamboo straws, the people who live here every day roll their eyes. The real talk at a useful dinner is about which local politicians actually deliver on waste contracts, which neighborhoods have functioning community recycling, and how to keep a small repair business alive when imported parts cost three times as much after the currency moves.
